## What is behind your knee pain?

The knee is a hinge between the thigh bone and the shin bone, with the kneecap sliding in a groove at the front. Cartilage covers the bone ends, and two rubbery pads called menisci sit between them. Strong ligaments hold it together and the thigh muscles control it.

Knee pain can come from any of these parts. Where it hurts, and what brings it on, gives the first clues.

### Runner's and sports knee

Pain around or behind the kneecap is common in runners, cyclists and people who play badminton, football or cricket. It often builds after a jump in training, more hills or a new pair of shoes. Sitting with bent knees, squatting and going down stairs can make it worse. Pain on the outer side of the knee in runners is often linked to the band of tissue running down the outside of the thigh.

A twist, a sudden stop or a tackle can injure a ligament or a meniscus. Swelling within hours, locking, or the knee giving way are signs that need a careful check.

### Age-related knee pain

From middle age, the cartilage in the knee can thin. You may feel stiffness after sitting, pain on walking longer distances, and some swelling after a busy day. Many people have these changes on an X-ray and still walk, travel and climb stairs with the right strength and pacing. Our page on <PageLink slug="arthritis-physiotherapy">arthritis physiotherapy</PageLink> explains how we approach joint wear in more detail.

### After knee replacement

If you have had or are planning a knee replacement, recovery follows your surgeon's protocol. Our page on <PageLink slug="post-surgery-rehabilitation">rehabilitation after knee replacement and other surgery</PageLink> covers it.

## When physiotherapy may help

Physiotherapy may help when knee pain limits walking, stairs, squatting, sport or sleep, when the knee feels weak or unstable, or when pain keeps returning each time you build up activity. It may help you understand what loads the knee and how to change that load. It cannot reverse wear in the joint, but it may help you move with less pain.

<RedFlags slug="knee-pain-physiotherapy">

- the knee is hot, red and swollen, especially with fever
- you cannot put weight on the leg after a fall or twist
- the knee looks out of shape, or the kneecap has moved out of place
- the knee locks and you cannot straighten it
- the calf is swollen, warm and painful, or you feel short of breath

A swollen, painful calf with breathlessness can be a blood clot. Get emergency care.

## How Smart Physio+ approaches knee pain

**Looking beyond the knee.** The knee sits between the hip and the foot, and both affect it. Dr. Nileema Chaudhary watches you walk, squat, step down and balance on one leg. She checks the strength of your thigh and hip muscles, how your foot moves, and how stable the ligaments are. She asks about your sport, footwear and training. She reads any scan or X-ray you bring.

**Treatment.** Your plan may include:

- strengthening for the thigh, hip and calf muscles, started at a level the knee accepts
- hands-on treatment and myofascial release for tight muscles around the knee and thigh
- kinesio taping to support the kneecap while you build strength
- changes to how much you run, jump or climb, so the knee recovers between sessions

## What to expect at your visits

Bring shorts and the shoes you walk or run in. If you have had a knee scan, bring the report.

We note a few measures at the start, such as how deep you can squat without pain or how many steps you manage. We repeat them at later visits so progress is clear. Home exercises are part of every plan. As the knee gets stronger, visits become less frequent and the home plan does more of the work.
