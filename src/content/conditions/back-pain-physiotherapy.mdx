## What is causing your back pain?

Lower back pain is pain between the bottom of your ribs and the top of your legs. It is one of the most common reasons people see a physiotherapist. It can start after lifting, bending or a long drive. It can also build up slowly with no clear cause.

In most people the pain comes from the muscles, joints, ligaments and discs of the lower back being strained or irritated. Doctors call this "mechanical" back pain. It means the pain changes with movement and position. It hurts, sometimes a lot, but it is usually not a sign of serious damage.

Common signs include:

- a dull ache or sharp catch across the lower back
- stiffness when you get out of bed or stand up from a chair
- pain that eases when you change position or walk a little
- muscle spasm that makes it hard to bend or turn

### Disc bulge

Between each pair of bones in your spine sits a disc. It works like a cushion. A disc bulge means the outer wall of the disc pushes outwards. Many people have a bulge on their scan and no pain at all. When a bulge does cause pain, it can feel deep in the back. If it presses on a nerve, pain may travel into the buttock or leg. Leg pain of that kind is called <PageLink slug="sciatica-physiotherapy">sciatica</PageLink>.

### Posture and desk-work pain

Long hours at a laptop, on a sofa or behind the wheel keep your back in one shape for too long. The muscles tire and the joints stiffen. You may notice the pain builds through the working day and eases at the weekend. There is no single correct posture. The aim is to move more often and to build enough strength that sitting is less tiring.

### Tailbone pain

The tailbone (coccyx) is the small bone at the very bottom of the spine. Tailbone pain is usually worse when you sit, especially on hard chairs, and when you stand up from sitting. It can follow a fall onto the bottom, childbirth or long periods of sitting. Sometimes there is no clear trigger.

## When physiotherapy may help

Physiotherapy may help when back pain stops you sitting, standing, walking, sleeping or working comfortably. It may also help when pain keeps coming back, or when you are unsure which movements are safe. A physiotherapist cannot promise a set result. What we can do is find what is making your back worse and give you a clear plan to work on it.

Some back pain needs a doctor before physiotherapy, or instead of it.

<RedFlags slug="back-pain-physiotherapy">

- loss of control of your bladder or bowels, or trouble passing urine
- numbness around your bottom, genitals or inner thighs (the "saddle" area)
- leg weakness that is getting worse, or both legs affected
- back pain after a major fall, accident or injury
- fever, unexplained weight loss, or a past history of cancer
- constant pain that is worse at night and does not ease with rest or position

If you have bladder or bowel changes or saddle numbness, go to a hospital emergency department straight away.

</RedFlags>

## How Smart Physio+ approaches back pain

**Assessment first.** Dr. Nileema Chaudhary asks when the pain started, what makes it better or worse and how it affects your day. She reads any scans or reports you bring. She then checks how your back bends and turns, how strong your hips and trunk are, and whether a nerve seems involved. You hear what she finds in plain words before any treatment starts.

**Treatment.** Your plan may include:

- hands-on treatment such as manual therapy and myofascial release to ease stiff joints and tight muscles
- kinesio taping for support during the first, more painful phase
- therapeutic exercise to get your back moving again
- advice on sleep, lifting and how to pace your work

### Robotic spine decompression

This is a padded table guided by a computer. It applies a slow, measured pull along your spine while you lie on it, then lets go, in cycles. It is considered for some people with disc-related pain once the assessment supports it. Most people feel a steady stretch. You can ask to stop at any time.

</Flagged>

**Rehabilitation.** As pain settles, the focus shifts to strength and confidence. Exercises progress from gentle movement to work that prepares you for your real day: lifting your child, a long commute, a gym session. The goal is to give you the tools to manage your own back.

## What to expect at your visits

Your first visit is mostly listening and examining. Bring any reports, X-rays or MRI scans, and a list of medicines. Wear clothes you can move in.

Before you leave, you know what we think is driving the pain, what the plan is, and a few exercises to start at home. At follow-up visits we recheck the movements that hurt at the start, so you can see what has changed. The plan moves forward when you are ready, not on a fixed timetable.

If something is not improving as expected, we say so and discuss other options, including seeing a doctor.
