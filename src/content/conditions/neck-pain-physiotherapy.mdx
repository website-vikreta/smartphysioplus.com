## Why does my neck hurt?

Your neck holds up a heavy head all day while letting it turn, nod and tilt. Seven small bones, the discs between them and many layers of muscle share that work. Pain starts when some of these tissues are overloaded, strained or stiff.

Most neck pain is not caused by serious injury. Common triggers are:

- long hours looking at a laptop or phone
- sleeping in an awkward position
- stress, which makes the shoulder and neck muscles tense up
- a sudden jolt, for example braking hard in traffic
- age-related wear in the neck joints and discs

You might notice a stiff neck that is hard to turn when reversing the car, aching at the base of the skull, or pain that spreads across the top of the shoulders.

### Screen and desk posture

Office workers, students and anyone who works from a laptop tend to drift forward towards the screen over the day. The head moves ahead of the shoulders, and the muscles at the back of the neck work harder to hold it up. By evening that can turn into a dull ache or a tight band across the neck.

### Upper back and shoulder-blade tension

Neck pain rarely stays in the neck. The muscles that lift and steady the shoulder blades attach to the neck. When they tire, you may feel a knot between the shoulder blades or a burning patch beside the spine. Stiffness in the upper back also makes the neck do more of the turning.

### Arm symptoms

Sometimes a nerve root in the neck is irritated. Then pain, tingling or numbness may spread into the shoulder, arm or hand. We test for this carefully at your assessment.

## When physiotherapy may help

Physiotherapy may help with neck pain that changes with movement and position, stiffness after sleep or desk work, tension headaches from the neck, and neck pain that keeps coming back. It may also help you return to driving, sport or work with less worry.

Some neck symptoms need a doctor before anything else.

<RedFlags slug="neck-pain-physiotherapy">

- neck pain after a fall, road accident or blow to the head
- weakness, clumsiness or numbness in both hands or arms
- trouble walking, or legs that feel unsteady or stiff
- double vision, difficulty speaking or swallowing, or drooping of the face
- a severe headache that came on suddenly, or comes with fever and a stiff neck
- unexplained weight loss or night pain that does not ease

Sudden dizziness, speech or vision changes, or face or arm weakness can be a stroke. Call an ambulance straight away.

## How Smart Physio+ approaches neck pain

**Assessment.** Dr. Nileema Chaudhary asks how your day looks: your screen time, your sleep, your stress and your sport. She checks how far your neck turns, tilts and bends, and which movements bring on the pain. She also checks your upper back, shoulder blades and shoulders. If you have arm symptoms, she tests the nerves to your arms. She reads any X-ray or MRI you bring.

**Treatment.** Your plan may include:

- gentle hands-on mobilisation of stiff neck and upper-back joints
- myofascial release for tight muscles across the shoulders
- kinesio taping to remind you of a better position during work
- exercises that build strength in the deep neck muscles and around the shoulder blades
- practical changes to your screen height, chair and phone use

**Keeping it away.** Once the pain eases, we build a short routine you can do at your desk and at home. The aim is a neck that copes with long days, not one that only feels good straight after treatment.

If dizziness or balance problems are part of your symptoms, we may suggest [vertigo and balance care with our neuro physiotherapy team](/neuro-physiotherapy/#vertigo-balance).

## What to expect at your visits

Wear a top that lets us see your neck and shoulders. If you work on a laptop, a photo of your desk set-up is useful.

You leave the first visit with an explanation of what we found and two or three exercises you can do anywhere. At later visits we retest neck movement and the activities that were hard at the start, so you can see progress. We adjust the plan as you improve and space out visits as you become more independent.
