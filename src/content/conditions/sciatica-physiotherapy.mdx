## What is sciatica?

Sciatica is not a diagnosis on its own. It is a name for pain that travels along the sciatic nerve. This large nerve starts from several nerve roots in the lower spine. It runs through the buttock and down the back of the leg to the foot.

When one of those roots is squeezed or inflamed, you can feel it far from the spine. The leg often hurts more than the back. The most common cause is a disc pressing on or irritating a nerve root. Narrowing of the space around the nerves can also cause it, especially in older adults.

People describe sciatica as:

- burning, shooting or electric pain down one leg
- pins and needles or numbness in the calf, foot or toes
- a heavy or weak feeling in the leg
- pain that flares when sitting, bending, coughing or sneezing

It usually affects one side. It is more common in middle age, and in people whose work involves long hours of driving or heavy lifting.

### How sciatica differs from back pain

Back pain stays mostly in the back. With sciatica the main problem is the nerve, so the leg symptoms matter most. This changes how we examine you and which exercises we choose. If your pain is mainly across the lower back, our page on <PageLink slug="back-pain-physiotherapy">physiotherapy for lower back pain</PageLink> fits you better.

## When physiotherapy may help

Many people with sciatica improve over weeks to months. Physiotherapy may help you stay active while that happens, ease how much the nerve is irritated, and rebuild strength in the leg. It is most useful when the leg pain changes with position or movement.

Nerve pain needs closer watching than ordinary back pain. Do not wait for a physiotherapy appointment in these cases.

- you cannot hold or pass urine normally, or you leak without knowing
- numbness spreads around the back passage, genitals or inner thighs
- your foot slaps the ground or your toes catch when you walk (foot drop)
- weakness in the leg is getting worse from day to day
- sciatica in both legs at the same time
- the pain started after a fall or road accident, or you also feel unwell with fever

## How Smart Physio+ approaches sciatica

**Checking the nerve.** Dr. Nileema Chaudhary first maps where your pain, tingling and numbness are. She then tests the nerve. She lifts your straight leg to see when symptoms start, checks your reflexes at the knee and ankle, tests skin sensation, and measures the strength of your foot, ankle and hip muscles. These checks show which nerve root is likely involved and how much it is affected. If your MRI or reports are available, she reads them alongside these findings.

**Easing the nerve.** Early treatment aims to calm the nerve rather than push through pain. Your plan may include:

- finding positions that take pressure off the nerve, for sleep and for sitting
- gentle movements that help the nerve glide, sometimes called nerve flossing
- hands-on treatment for the lower back and buttock muscles
- simple changes to how you sit, drive and get out of bed

**Building back up.** Once the leg pain is less sharp, we add strength work for the trunk, hips and legs. We bring back the activities you avoided, one at a time.

## What to expect at your visits

Expect the first visit to include a full nerve check, so wear shorts or loose trousers. Bring any MRI report and the names of medicines your doctor has given.

At each follow-up we repeat the key nerve checks. We look for the leg pain moving up towards the back, which is often a good sign. We also check that strength and reflexes are holding steady. If they get worse, we will tell you and help you see a spine doctor.

Home work is short and specific: two or three movements, done a few times a day. We change them as your symptoms change.
