## What causes shoulder pain?

The shoulder moves more than any other joint in the body. That freedom comes from a shallow ball-and-socket joint held in place mostly by muscles, not bone. Four small muscles, called the rotator cuff, wrap around the ball and keep it centred while bigger muscles lift the arm. When these muscles or their tendons are overloaded, pain follows.

You may feel pain at the front or side of the shoulder, often spreading to the upper arm. Reaching up to a shelf, putting on a shirt, fastening a bra or lying on that side are typical triggers.

### Rotator cuff and overuse pain

Rotator cuff pain is common in people who lift overhead, at the gym or at work, in swimmers and badminton players, and in people over 40 whose tendons are less able to cope with a sudden jump in activity. The tendon becomes irritated rather than torn in most cases. A painful arc, where lifting the arm hurts in the middle of the movement but eases near the top, is a typical sign.

A fall onto the arm can tear a tendon. If you cannot lift your arm after a fall, see a doctor.

### Posture-related shoulder pain

Long hours with rounded shoulders at a desk or on a phone change how the shoulder blade sits. The space at the top of the shoulder becomes more crowded when you lift the arm. Tight chest muscles and a stiff upper back add to this. The neck can also refer pain to the shoulder, so we always check it.

### Frozen shoulder

Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis) is a painful stiffening of the joint capsule. It is more common between 40 and 60 and in people with diabetes or thyroid problems. It usually moves through a painful phase, a stiff phase and a slow thawing phase. Physiotherapy may help you manage pain and keep as much movement as possible through each phase. We set the pace of stretching by how irritable the shoulder is that week.

## When physiotherapy may help

Physiotherapy may help when shoulder pain stops you reaching, lifting, dressing, working out or sleeping, and when it has lasted more than a few days without improving. It is often the first treatment for rotator cuff pain. It may also help after a doctor has given an injection, to rebuild strength while the pain is lower.

<RedFlags slug="shoulder-pain-physiotherapy">

- you cannot lift the arm at all after a fall or injury
- the shoulder looks out of place or deformed
- the shoulder is hot, red and swollen, or you have a fever
- pain in the left shoulder or arm with chest pain, breathlessness or sweating
- a lump, unexplained weight loss, or constant night pain

Shoulder or arm pain with chest tightness or breathlessness can be a heart problem. Call an ambulance.

## How Smart Physio+ approaches shoulder pain

**Assessment.** Dr. Nileema Chaudhary watches you lift, reach behind your back and rotate the arm. She tests the strength of each rotator cuff muscle, checks how the shoulder blade moves, and examines your neck and upper back. She also asks about your work, sport and sleep. Any scan reports you bring are part of the picture.

**Treatment.** Your plan may include:

- graded exercises for the rotator cuff and shoulder blade muscles, starting below the pain threshold
- hands-on treatment and myofascial release for tight chest, neck and upper back muscles
- mobilisation of a stiff upper back so the shoulder does not do all the work
- kinesio taping to support shoulder blade position during the day
- advice on sleeping positions and on how to modify gym or work tasks for now

**Building capacity.** Tendons get stronger with gradually heavier load. Over the following weeks the exercises move from gentle holds to lifting with weights or bands, and then to the movements of your sport or job.

## What to expect at your visits

Wear a vest or a loose top so we can see your shoulder blade move. Bring any ultrasound or MRI report.

Before you leave we explain what we think is causing the pain and give you two or three exercises. At later visits we retest your range of movement, strength and the tasks that were hard at the start. Shoulder tendons are slow to change, so we explain what progress to expect and adjust the plan at each step.
