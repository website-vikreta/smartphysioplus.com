## What is arthritis?

Arthritis means a joint that is painful and stiff because of changes inside it. The most common type is osteoarthritis. Over time the smooth cartilage that lines the bone ends thins, the bone underneath reacts, and the joint lining can become irritated. Knees, hips, hands and the spine are affected most often.

Arthritis is not simply "wear and tear" that only gets worse. Joints are living tissue. Many people with arthritis on an X-ray have good days and bad days, and the way they move and load the joint changes how it feels.

Typical signs include:

- stiffness first thing in the morning or after sitting, which eases once you get moving
- aching after a long walk, a day on your feet or stairs
- a joint that feels swollen, creaky or less steady
- flare-ups, where the joint is more painful for a few days

Other types, such as rheumatoid arthritis and gout, are driven by the immune system or by crystals in the joint. They need a doctor's diagnosis and medicines. Physiotherapy may support that care.

### Arthritis and getting older

Arthritis is more common as we age, but it is not something you have to accept quietly. Older adults often tell us their real worry is losing independence: getting up from a low chair, using Indian-style toilets, walking to the temple or the market, travelling to see family. Those everyday goals shape the plan. Balance work is part of it, because stiff joints and weaker legs raise the risk of a fall.

## When physiotherapy may help

Physiotherapy may help you keep joints moving, build the muscles that protect them, manage flare-ups and stay active. It is recommended in most arthritis guidance as a core part of care. It may also help you prepare for joint surgery if you and your surgeon decide on it. It will not change what an X-ray shows.

<RedFlags slug="arthritis-physiotherapy">

- a joint becomes hot, red and very swollen within a day, especially with fever
- you suddenly cannot bear weight on a hip or knee
- several joints swell at once, with morning stiffness lasting over an hour
- pain after a fall, particularly in the hip or wrist of an older adult
- unexplained weight loss, night sweats or feeling generally unwell

A single hot, swollen joint with fever can be an infection. See a doctor the same day.

## How Smart Physio+ approaches arthritis

**Assessment.** Dr. Nileema Chaudhary asks which tasks have become hard and what you would like to do again. She checks how each affected joint moves, the strength of the muscles around it, your walking and your balance. She reads your X-rays, blood test results and any notes from your doctor or rheumatologist.

**Treatment.** Your plan may include:

- strengthening exercises, often the most helpful single step for arthritic knees and hips
- range-of-movement exercises to ease stiffness
- gentle hands-on treatment for stiff joints and tight surrounding muscles
- balance training to help prevent falls
- advice on pacing, walking aids, footwear and setting up your home

**Managing flare-ups.** We agree a simple plan for bad days: which exercises to keep, which to drop, and when to call us. Knowing what to do takes much of the worry out of a flare.

## What to expect at your visits

Bring your X-ray films or reports, a list of your medicines and, if possible, a family member. Wear comfortable clothes and the shoes you normally walk in.

We record a few simple tests at the start, such as how quickly you can stand up from a chair five times, or how far you walk comfortably. We repeat them later so you can see change. Exercises are kept short and realistic so they fit into your day. Over time the aim is for you to manage the joint yourself, with check-ins when you need them.
