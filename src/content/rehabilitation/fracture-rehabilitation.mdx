## What happens after a fracture heals

A broken bone is usually held still while it heals, in a plaster cast, a brace, or with a plate, screws or rods put in during surgery. Keeping it still lets the bone knit. The price is that everything around it gets stiff and weak.

When the cast comes off, many people are surprised by what they find. The joint above or below the break barely moves. The muscles look thinner. The skin may be dry and the limb swollen. Walking, gripping or lifting feel strange. This is expected, and it is what fracture rehabilitation works on.

Common fractures we may see include the wrist after a fall onto an outstretched hand, the ankle after a twist, and the shoulder or upper arm in older adults.

## When rehabilitation may help

Rehabilitation may help once your orthopaedic doctor says the bone is healed enough to start moving and loading it. It may help with stiffness, weakness, swelling, a limp, poor balance and the fear of putting weight through the injured part again.

<RedFlags slug="fracture-rehabilitation">

- pain at the fracture site that is getting worse instead of better
- the limb looks bent or out of shape, or a new bump appears
- a hot, red or leaking surgical wound, or a fever
- a swollen, painful calf, or breathlessness, after a leg fracture
- numbness, pins and needles or colour change in the fingers or toes

Severe, increasing pain with a tight, swollen limb needs emergency care the same day.

## How Smart Physio+ approaches fracture rehabilitation

**Assessment.** Dr. Nileema Chaudhary reads your X-rays and your orthopaedic doctor's notes on how the bone has healed and what loading is allowed. She measures how far each nearby joint moves, how strong the muscles are, and how much swelling there is. For a leg injury she watches you walk.

**Treatment.** Your plan may include:

- movement exercises for the stiff joints near the fracture
- hands-on mobilisation and soft-tissue work, within what healing allows
- strengthening, starting light and building as the bone gets stronger
- swelling control and advice on raising the limb
- walking practice and balance work after leg fractures

**Getting back to normal use.** The final stage is practice with the real tasks: typing and cooking after a wrist fracture, stairs and uneven ground after an ankle fracture, reaching overhead after a shoulder fracture.

## What to expect at your visits

Bring your latest X-ray, the doctor's notes and any brace or splint you were given. Tell us the date of the injury and of any surgery.

We measure movement and strength at the start and recheck them as you go. You get a short home routine to do every day, because stiff joints respond best to frequent, gentle work between visits.
