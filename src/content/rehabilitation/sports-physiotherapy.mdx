## What counts as a sports injury?

A sports injury is any injury that happens during exercise or that exercise keeps aggravating. It does not matter whether you are a state-level athlete or someone who joined a gym last month.

Sports injuries fall into two broad groups.

**Sudden injuries** happen in a moment: a rolled ankle on the football ground, a hamstring that pulls in a sprint, a shoulder that gives during a dive, a twisted knee when landing badly in badminton. They often come with swelling, bruising and a clear "this is when it happened".

**Overuse injuries** creep up. Shin pain that appears after you add distance to your runs, an Achilles tendon that is stiff every morning, a shoulder that aches after every bowling spell, an elbow that hurts after long gym sessions. They usually follow a change: more training, a new surface, new shoes, or less recovery.

## When sports physiotherapy may help

Sports physiotherapy may help you understand what went wrong, settle the injury, stay as fit as possible while it heals, and return to your sport in planned steps. It may also help when the same injury keeps coming back each season.

<RedFlags slug="sports-physiotherapy">

- you heard a pop and could not continue, with rapid swelling
- you cannot put weight on the leg or use the arm after the injury
- a joint looks deformed or out of place
- a blow to the head with headache, confusion, vomiting or drowsiness
- calf pain and swelling after a long flight or a period of not moving

Any head injury with confusion, repeated vomiting or worsening headache needs urgent medical care. Do not return to play the same day.

## How Smart Physio+ approaches sports injuries

**Assessment.** Dr. Nileema Chaudhary asks about your sport, your position or event, your training week and what changed before the injury. She examines the injured area and the joints above and below it. She watches you do movements close to your sport, such as hopping, landing, throwing or a squat, as far as the injury allows.

**Treatment.** Your plan may include:

- hands-on treatment and sports massage for tight, overworked muscles
- kinesio taping to support an area while you keep training
- strengthening and control exercises for the injured area and the chain around it
- advice on load: what to keep, what to change and what to pause

### Load management

Most overuse injuries come from training load rising faster than the body can adapt. We look at your recent weeks and help you build back with a plan you can follow: how much, how often and how hard, with simple rules for when to ease off.

### Return to play

Returning is a process, not a date. It usually moves through stages: pain-free daily life, full strength, sport-specific drills, training with your team or group, and then full competition. Before each step we check that you are ready. If you have had surgery, such as a ligament reconstruction, this follows your surgeon's timeline and links with our <PageLink slug="post-surgery-rehabilitation">post-surgery rehabilitation</PageLink>.

## What to expect at your visits

Bring your training kit, the shoes you train in, and any scans or reports. If you track your training on an app or a watch, bring that too.

At the first visit we agree your goal, for example a race date or the start of the season, and the tests that will show you are ready. Each visit rechecks those tests. Your home programme changes as you progress, and we keep it short enough to fit around training.
